# Writing emails in the user's voice

The user's emails are short, direct, and charismatic. The reader knows what is wanted or what happened right after the greeting, the explanation only justifies the point, and the ending leaves a good feeling.

## Structure

Every email follows this order:

1. **Greeting** - short, matched to the recipient, with a warm cue ("Good morning Anna," / "Hi Anna," / "Dear Mr. Keller,"). Name only, no small talk.
2. **The point** - one or two sentences stating the request, the decision, or the result. Say it plainly. Skip openers like "I hope you are well" or "I am writing to...".
3. **The why** - one to three sentences with only the reasoning the reader needs to act or accept. Put a deadline or next step here if there is one.
4. **Warm close** - one short, sincere line that ends on a good tone. Take it from the sweet-spot closers below and vary it so it never feels stamped on.
5. **Sign-off and name** - a warm sign-off word, then the user's first name if known from context. If the name is unknown, stop after the sign-off word.

The reader decides fastest when the point comes first and the reason second, so the order stays fixed even for bad news: result, then reason, then a constructive close.

## Charisma: warmth plus competence

Charisma means every email carries both kinds of cues. Warm cues make the reader feel connected and safe; competence cues make the reader trust that things will get done. An email with only competence cues reads as cold and bossy, and one with only warmth cues reads as fluffy. Aim for both in every email, without adding length - swap neutral words for cue words rather than adding sentences.

**Warm cues:** we, our, us, both, together, happy, glad, excited, thrilled, collaborate, connect.
**Competence cues:** goal, outcome, plan, next step, forward, prepare, solve, power through, effective, productive.

Pair them: state the shared goal ("our goal", "so we can both..."), name the concrete next step, and use "we/our" wherever it is true.

**Sweet-spot greetings** (warm and competent): "Good morning team!", "Excited for today.", "Welcome everyone.", "Can't wait to get started together."

**Sweet-spot closing lines:** "Excited to work with you.", "Looking forward to this.", "Happy to answer any questions.", "We got this!", "To your success.", "Great work.", "Thank you for everything."

**Sign-off words:** use warm ones ("Best,", "Cheers,", "Warmly,", "Warmest regards,") or a sweet-spot phrase ("Looking forward to this,"). Competence-only words ("Regards,", "Sincerely,", "Respectfully,", "Appreciatively,") are acceptable only in strictly formal or legal contexts.

**German emails:** always close with "Viele Grüße" (no comma after it, per German convention), regardless of formality. Use it in place of any other sign-off word when writing in German.

**Avoid (these make the email feel dismissive):** a bare "Hi" with no name, "What's up?", "Been busy?", and sign-offs like "Bye,", "Talk soon,", "See you soon,", or no sign-off at all.

The cues are English; when writing in another language, use the natural equivalents of the same warm and competent words. Keep the tone sincere: at most one exclamation mark, and never stack several cue phrases in one sentence.

## Style

- Aim for 40-90 words in total. Go past 120 only when the content truly requires it.
- Use plain words, active voice, and first person. Cut filler, stacked hedges ("just", "maybe", "I was wondering if"), and repeated thanks.
- Write in the language of the recipient or the thread. If neither is clear, use the language the user wrote their request in. Match formality to the relationship (for example du/Sie, first name/surname).
- Keep to one main ask per email. If there are more, lead with the most important and give each other item one short line.

## Workflow

1. Identify the recipient, the point, and the reason. If the point itself is unclear, ask one short question; otherwise draft straight away and note any assumption in a single line after the draft.
2. When replying to an existing email, read it first and answer its question in the point.
3. Write the draft. For a new email, add a subject line that mirrors the point in eight words or fewer.
4. Check before delivering: Is the point visible in the first two lines after the greeting? Is the why three sentences or fewer? Is there at least one warm cue and one competence cue? Does the closing line come from the sweet-spot list, and does the sign-off avoid the dismissive list? Cut everything else.
5. Return the email only, ready to copy. No commentary beyond a brief assumption note.

If the user shares real emails of theirs or corrects a draft, treat that as the source of truth over this file and match it.

## Good vs. bad example

Input: Ask Marta to send the Q3 numbers by Friday, I need them for the board deck.

**Good**
```
Subject: Q3 numbers by Friday

Good morning Marta,

Could you send me the Q3 numbers by Friday? Our goal is a finished board deck on Monday, and those figures are the last missing piece.

Happy to answer any questions - excited to see this come together!

Best,
[Name]
```
Why it works: greeting with a warm cue; the ask comes first; "our goal" and "last missing piece" combine shared purpose with a concrete outcome; the closing line offers help and ends on a good tone; the sign-off is warm.

**Bad**
```
Marta,

Send me the Q3 numbers by Friday. Need them for the board deck.

Regards,
[Name]
```
Why it fails: no warm greeting, and the request reads as an order; there is no "we" or shared goal, so it has competence at most and no connection; the closing line is missing and the competence-only sign-off ends the email flat.

## More examples

**Result**
Input: Tell Jonas the contract is signed, legal cleared it yesterday.
Output:
```
Subject: Contract signed

Hi Jonas,

The contract is signed and we are good to go. Legal cleared the last open point yesterday, so nothing is blocking our start on the 1st.

Can't wait to get started together!

Warmly,
[Name]
```

**Decline**
Input: Decline the vendor's meeting request, budget is frozen until January.
Output:
```
Subject: Meeting request

Hi Tom,

I will have to pass on the meeting for now. Our budget is frozen until January, so we cannot take on new vendors before then.

Happy to reconnect in the new year - thank you for thinking of us!

Best,
[Name]
```
